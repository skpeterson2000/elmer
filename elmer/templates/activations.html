Parks and summits

Parks on the Air and Summits on the Air are what get most people to carry a radio somewhere. Almost every wasted trip is a planning failure rather than a radio one — the wrong kit, or the wrong side of a contour — and that is fixable at a table days early, for nothing.

What counts

Both programmes are run by people who change their own rules, so each of these says which document it came from and the day somebody read it. That is the one part of this page that goes stale by another body's decision rather than by physics.

Whose land it is

Neither programme can give you permission to be somewhere. The park's own rules come first, and those are written by whoever owns the ground. What the regulations actually say, quoted with the section, so it can be checked — and the one pattern that runs through all of them: ask, nothing driven into a tree, no noise, nothing left behind, and a club outing is an event.
