The station identifying itself, at twenty words a minute, once when ELMER opens. It is a plain way of hearing that the unit is up and the sound works — and in a room where somebody else knows the program, it is an invitation to a game. {% if announce.de %}Your name goes out with it, because you are a supporter who asked to be named: the room hears whose unit this is. {% else %}Supporters who ask to be named have their own callsign keyed after ELMER’s, so the room hears whose unit it is.{% endif %} Turn it off where it would not be welcome — a net in progress, a classroom, a field site at night. Being quiet is sometimes what being in a community asks of a station.
On a unit running as an appliance it goes out the moment ELMER opens. In an ordinary browser it waits for your first click on the page, because a browser keeps audio silent until a page has been touched and that is the browser’s rule rather than ELMER’s.

The Marine Radio Operator Permit, the GROL and the Ship Radar endorsement — three more cards on the dashboard and a second rank. Off the front by default; the pools are always there.

Your own, made for ELMER under My Account → API apps at RepeaterBook.com (ELMER has to be on RepeaterBook’s list of apps for that page to offer it). With it, the amateur and GMRS repeaters for the state your QTH is in are fetched straight from RepeaterBook under your account, kept on this unit for your own use, and asked for again when the QTH moves to another state or a month has passed. The token goes to RepeaterBook and nowhere else, and once saved it is never shown again, here or anywhere. Without one, ELMER reads TowerWitch’s list or an import.

ELMER is a gift to the amateur radio community, free for everyone. Somebody who thanks the developer with a cup of coffee at github.com/sponsors/skpeterson2000 and says in the note what they would like printed — a callsign, a name, a club — gets an eight-character key back, cut for that name. With it ELMER says thank you instead of asking, and a hall this unit plays in can put the name on the card between rounds — only if the box above is ticked. The key opens nothing and its absence closes nothing.
