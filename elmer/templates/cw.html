CW

Learn it, copy it, send it — and decode what is coming out of the receiver.
Characters are always sent at the character speed; lowering the effective speed stretches the gaps between them, not the characters themselves (Farnsworth). Learning a slowed-down character teaches a sound you have to unlearn later. Pick whatever tone you hear most comfortably — anywhere from 300 to 1200 Hz — and it is remembered.
Koch method
two characters at full speed, then one more at a time

Start with two characters sent at the speed you eventually want to copy. When you are copying them at 90% or better, add the next. It feels brutal for a day and then it works, because you are learning the sound of each character rather than counting dots.

Decode off air

Point a microphone at the receiver's speaker. ELMER locks onto the loudest tone in the CW range and decodes the timing. It works on a clean, steady signal; QRM, QSB and a swinging fist all degrade it, exactly as they do for every other decoder. It works out the sending speed from what it hears, so expect the first character or two to garble while it finds the speed — then it locks on.
