Reading it

Moonbounce is line of sight to a target a quarter of a million miles off, so the only question of geography is whether both stations can point at it at once. That is the window, and it is why an EME schedule with Europe from the middle of America is an afternoon affair and Japan an evening one: the moon rises for them hours before it does for you.

The color is geometry only. What it does not show, and what changes the day as much: the moon's distance, which moves the path loss about 2 dB between perigee and apogee (the loss goes as the fourth power, there and back); its declination, because a moon low in the south sits in front of the Milky Way and the sky noise behind it can double; and how far it is from the sun, whose noise gets into any beam within about fifteen degrees. Those three are in the figures above and in the verdict, and they are the same for everybody on Earth at once.

Two stations low to the horizon at each end are in the worst of it: ground noise, trees, the neighbours, and a signal that has to go through the most air on the way out and back. Most of the contacts made happen with both ends above 20 degrees, which is what green means.
