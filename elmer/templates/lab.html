Ionospheric hop — why a band "opens" and where the skip zone falls
MUF = foF2 × sec(angle of incidence at the layer), which allows for the curve of the earth — that is why the M-factor tops out near 3.4 rather than running away at low takeoff angles. Push the frequency past the MUF for a given angle and the ray stops bending back: it leaves through the layer.

The height is adjustable because the layer moves. The F2 peak sits roughly 250–400 km up, lower and denser by day, higher and thinner at night, and it shifts with season, latitude and solar activity. You do not judge it by ear — it is measured, by ionosondes: a radar pointed straight up that sweeps frequency and times the echo, reporting foF2 and the peak height hmF2. "Use a real measurement" pulls the nearest station's current figures in.

Smith chart — what the feedline does to your antenna

The chart is the reflection coefficient plane. Every impedance, divided by the line's own Z0, lands somewhere inside the circle: the centre is a perfect match, the rim is total reflection. The circles running left to right are constant resistance; the arcs curving off them are constant reactance, inductive above the axis and capacitive below. Move the antenna and watch where it goes; add feedline and watch it travel.

Positive is inductive — the antenna is long for the frequency. Negative is capacitive, and it is short.

RF exposure evaluation — required of every station

Since 2021 every amateur station must evaluate its RF exposure and be able to show the result — 47 CFR 97.13(c), against the limits in 47 CFR 1.1310. Enter each band you actually run, and ELMER produces a station record you can print and post in the shack.

Bands and antennas
Power density is estimated in the far field with a ground reflection factor of 1.6 on field strength, the conservative form OET Bulletin 65 recommends for amateur work. Distances inside the near field are flagged rather than hidden. This is your own evaluation — it is not filed with the FCC and carries no approval, but it is what you are required to have done.
