HF bands — the wall chart
N0NBH's ratings, as they appear on every shack wall. Eight figures in all: four band groups, a daytime rating and a night-time one, worked out from the flux and the field. They are not hourly, they are not for your location, and one word has to cover a whole group — on this evening's numbers 30 m is under the MUF and 20 m is over it, and the group they share can only be right about one of them. The band plan asks the same question band by band and hour by hour, against the measured critical frequency, and where the two disagree that is usually why.

Take it to the pool

Every number above is something the exams ask about. Drill the propagation sections while the conditions are in front of you — it sticks far better than reading the answer key.

T3A — radio wave characteristics T3C — propagation modes G3A — solar indices G3B — MUF, LUF, paths G3C — ionospheric layers E3A — EME, meteor scatter E3B — trans-equatorial, long path E3C — aurora, ducting, MUF

Solar and geomagnetic data from N0NBH (hamqsl.com) and NOAA SWPC, cached for 15 minutes; ionosonde readings from the GIRO/Digisonde network via prop.kc2g.com. The MUF and foF2 are a teaching model of flux, sun angle and latitude, scaled to meet whatever sondes are in range — the level comes from them and the shape from the sun. Where the tiles above say how many stations that was, they are corrected; where they say Est., nothing was in range and the model is standing on its own. Either way it is a model of the average ionosphere, not of your antenna, your power or the far end: read it to see how the physics moves, not to plan a schedule.
