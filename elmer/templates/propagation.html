HF bands — the wall chart
N0NBH's ratings, as they appear on every shack wall. Eight figures in all: four band groups, a daytime rating and a night-time one, worked out from the flux and the field. They are not hourly, they are not for your location, and one word has to cover a whole group — on this evening's numbers 30 m is under the MUF and 20 m is over it, and the group they share can only be right about one of them. The band plan asks the same question band by band and hour by hour, against the measured critical frequency, and where the two disagree that is usually why. 11 m is CB — no license, forty channels — rated like its neighbours because it opens with them.

Calibrate my forecast — a year of the sky, run blind
{# The forecast's level is wrong by season, and by how much depends on where the operator stands. So rather than ship a guess at the winter anomaly for every latitude, the unit measures its own: it fetches a year of readings from the ionosondes nearest it, runs the forecast blind over that year hour by hour, and fits the correction by month and by sky. Five minutes on a Pi, and the operator watches it happen - a finding a month, between cards - because a screen that goes quiet for five minutes reads as broken. #}

The forecast knows the sun and the flux; it does not know that the F layer over you runs denser on a winter noon than the sun angle says, or by how much. The sondes nearest you know. This fetches the last year of their readings, runs ELMER’s own forecast blind over that year — each hour given only what it would have known then — and fits the correction month by month and sky by sky, for this place. Then it runs the year again with the correction on, so you see what it bought.

Three depths. Each one refreshes the months it covers and leaves the rest as the last run that saw them — so a quick run in September keeps the December a full year measured. It needs the network for the first minute, and you are not forgotten while it runs: it tells you what it finds, month by month, with a card or two in between. Nothing leaves this unit. The record it reads is GIRO’s and GFZ’s, and it says so. The model already carries the season as measured at the sondes between 30 and 45 N, so a unit in the middle of North America may be told there is little to correct — which is a measured answer too.

    Solar and geomagnetic data from N0NBH (hamqsl.com) and NOAA SWPC, cached for 15 minutes; ionosonde readings from the GIRO/Digisonde network via prop.kc2g.com. The MUF and foF2 are a teaching model of flux, sun angle and latitude, scaled to meet whatever sondes are in range — the level comes from them and the shape from the sun. Where the tiles above say how many stations that was, they are corrected; where they say Est., nothing was in range and the model is standing on its own. Either way it is a model of the average ionosphere, not of your antenna, your power or the far end: read it to see how the physics moves, not to plan a schedule.
