And what the antenna can be made of

The antenna is the half of the station that can still be built out of what is standing around you. These are the ones ELMER has real numbers for:

Those are examples and not the list — they are simply the ones this program can put a figure on. Nothing settles a candidate except whether it conducts, whether you can get it up and clear of things, and whether it is the right length. And a piece of metal that fails all three can still be the ground, the counterpoise, the reflector or the mast, which is where most of what is lying about turns out to be useful.

None of it has to be guessed at. With a NanoVNA on hand, anything that conducts can be swept and asked directly: it gives the reactance with its sign, so it says both whether the thing is resonant where you need it and which way to cut. Reading that is a skill, and it is the skill this program is here to hand over — the Lab teaches it with an instrument or without one, and the antenna calculator will cut any material above to length.
